# Covering the strut's wire channel and the tube end (2026-11-17)

Owner's idea: split the arm into a 2 mm pocketed base and a 1 mm **skin on the inboard face**, so the wiring is hidden and the open end of the spindle tube (the square hole, the back of the gearmotor) is closed flush. Owner's conditions: investigate **screws plus a gasket**; **epoxy only if the part never has to be opened**, and in that case **rigid conductor rails instead of wires**. Numbers: `hardware/strut/cover_study.py` (plain python3); **the model is in `hardware/strut/strut.py` (`COVER = True`)**, built 2026-11-17 (section "As built" below, which supersedes the numbers in Option 1 where they differ). Tags as elsewhere: ASM assumption, ESTIMATE my arithmetic.

## What the geometry already gives us
- The tube starts at the collar's inboard face and the motor's tail is 4 mm inside it (`strut.py`: the motor is flush with the plate, the collar stands 4 mm proud). **A skin over the collar end therefore needs no change to the motor or the tube**: it closes a 4 mm pocket in front of the motor tail, where the leads can turn toward the notch and channel (this corrects my earlier remark that the motor would move 1 mm deeper).
- On strut B (outer) the inboard face of boss and plate are one flat plane, so one skin covers the whole arm. On strut A (inner) the pivot boss also stands 4 mm proud of the plate, and the arm faces the body wall at 3.25 mm: its inboard side is hardly visible. A skin on A is for parts commonality; B is where it matters.

## Option 1: screws and a gasket (serviceable; my recommendation)
- **Gasket:** a 0.5 mm silicone cord in a 0.65 x 0.40 mm groove cut in the base round the channel and the tip pocket (squeeze 20%, groove 76% full), with the skin landing on bare metal. The metal-to-metal stop sets the squeeze, so it does not depend on screw torque. A flat die-cut gasket under a 1 mm skin would depend on torque and is not recommended.
- **Screws:** M1.6 A2, about 19 per strut (two rows at 9 mm pitch beside the groove, six round the tip cap, three at the pivot), 76 for the rover, about 9 g. Thread engagement in the 2 mm base is 1.25 d, enough for a cover (it carries no structural load). Heads: a 1 mm skin is only just deep enough for a countersunk M1.6, so use low-head screws in shallow counterbores, or take the skin to 1.2 mm (the base stays at 2). The rows of small heads match the bezel's six screws and the lid cams' round heads.
- **Cost:** the skin is no longer part of the section. The arm's bending stiffness to a sideways knock falls to **32% of the monolithic 3 mm plate** (the two layers slide), which is a tip deflection of 0.20 mm against 0.06 mm for 5 N (ASM), and a root stress of 13 MPa (factor 21 on yield). Fine at this size, but it is a real loss of stiffness, and out-of-plane stiffness is the weak direction. The vertical (in-plane) strength that carries the wheel is unchanged.
- **Serviceable:** lift the skin, replace a motor lead, put it back with a new cord.

## Option 2: bonded skin with rigid rails (permanent)
- Bonded, the section acts as one: **97% of the monolithic stiffness** (0.07 mm, 7 MPa); no screws or gasket; the cleanest finish. The cost is that nothing inside can ever be repaired: a failed wheel motor means a new strut.
- **Rails:** a 0.8 mm FR-4 strip with two 1.0 mm traces fits a 3.0 x 1.0 mm channel, with solder pads at the motor end and the pivot end (the pivot end meets the hub shaft's cross hole, soldered once). Two ounce copper: 39 mohm out and back over 80 mm, 63 mV and 0.10 W at the motor's assumed stall current (1.6 A, ESTIMATE; **check the #3068's stall current against its datasheet**), ampacity 4 A (IPC-2221, 10 degree rise); one ounce also works (79 mohm, 2.4 A) but with less margin. The rail is also the insulator and the connection point; the motor's own leads are the only wire left, and it is short and inside the tube pocket.
- The motor can still be replaced if the **tube end** stays removable: bond the skin to the arm only, and close the 4 mm pocket with a small separate flush screwed cap (the cap makes the one exception to "never opened").

## Recommendation
**Option 1, with the cord gasket and low-head M1.6 screws, and the skin at 1.2 mm.** Reasons: the motors and leads are the part most likely to need service (the heat path through the bore is an untested estimate), the stiffness loss is acceptable, and a prototype that cannot be opened is a bad choice before anything has been measured. Revisit Option 2 once the drive has run for a long time; the rail channel is the same, so the base part does not change.

## As built (2026-11-17, owner chose Option 1 and the skin on the inboard face)
`strut.py` with `COVER = True`; exports `out/strut_covers.step` (skins and caps), `out/strut_cover_screws.step`; the Blender export and render draw them (`VIEW=inboard NOBODY=on` shows the faces).

- **The skin lives inside the 3 mm plate, not on top of it.** My first model added a 1.2 mm skin outside the plate; it hit strut A's plate where the two arms cross, because the layers are only 1.0 mm apart. So the plate keeps its faces and its 3 mm: the **base is 1.8 mm** and the **skin is 1.2 mm**, cut from the plate's inboard layer (arm outline from 15.5 mm out of the pivot to the tip collar). Result: 0 mm3 overlap between the cover parts and the struts, the motors, the spindles, the bearings, the wheels, the body and the hub parts; the A-versus-B check is unchanged at 12 of 144 angle pairs with the skins and caps included.
- **Channel:** 3.0 x 1.0 mm in the base's face under the skin, from the tip to the pivot. On strut B it continues across the boss to the hub bore, open on the inboard face inside 15.5 mm, under strut A's boss face (1 mm gap, hidden); on strut A it ends at the skin edge, facing the 3 mm gap to the body wall. **The collar notch is gone:** the wires leave the motor along its pivot-side flat and cross the tube wall through a 1.5 x 1.0 mm slot (at the channel's depth) straight into the channel, so nothing is open on the inboard side.
- **Gasket:** two cord grooves (0.65 x 0.40 mm, 2.4 mm either side of the centre line) along the channel, ending at the collar and open at the pivot end (the wires go on to the hub there). **The cap's seal and the cord's ends are not drawn**; the cap is expected to seal with a flat silicone ring (ASM, not modelled).
- **Screws:** 10 M1.6 per skin (five a side, 8.5 mm pitch, 2.6 mm in from the edge) and 3 per cap (at 135, 225 and 270 degrees round the tip, clear of the clamp slit and screw); 52 for the rover, about 6 g. **The thread engagement is short: 1.4 mm (0.87 d) in the 1.8 mm base, a stripping capacity of about 440 N (ESTIMATE), so tighten to 0.1 N.m** and treat a thicker base as the fix if the screws strip in use.
- **Cap:** a 24 mm disc 1.2 mm thick on the collar's inboard end face (so the collar plus cap is 5.2 mm, its outline unchanged and the tube end closed). Strut A's cap stands 2.1 mm from the body wall.
- **Mass:** the four skins and caps are 21.4 g, the screws about 6 g; the four struts fall from 98 g to 81 g (the skin layer and the deeper channel are removed), so the net is **+10 g** against the 98 g of the old struts (ESTIMATE, 6061 at 2.70 g/cm3; spindle tubes 10.7 g and 9.9 g unchanged apart from the slot).
- **Stiffness:** with the base at 1.8 mm and the skin at 1.2 mm, the screwed arm is **27%** of the solid plate's bending stiffness to a sideways knock (tip 0.24 mm for 5 N, ASM, root stress 14 MPa, factor 19 on yield). The owner's point stands: the main loads on the strut are in its own plane, which the layers do not affect.
- **Not done:** the cord and the cap's ring are not drawn; the pivot-end wire path for strut A (it leaves the channel at the skin edge into the 3 mm gap, then to a body-wall hole: not modelled); the clamp check `pivot_checks.py` was not re-run (the collar and tube are unchanged, the tube wall has a 1.5 mm slot at the pivot side near the clamp, which the check does not know about).
