## 2. Wheel counting: back-EMF (owner, 2026-11-13)

The wheel motors carry no encoders, and there is no room for one in the 10 mm motor envelope, so wheel travel is counted from the motor's back-EMF.

- **Method (assumed, to be worked out):** the H-bridge briefly opens (a short coast gap each PWM period), the controller reads the motor terminal voltage, and the speed follows from it (EMF is proportional to speed). Counts come from integrating speed over time.
- **Consequences to model (assumed):** poor accuracy at low speed and near stall, a direction sign taken from the drive command, and drift from the motor constant changing with temperature and battery sag. This is odometry error the sim does not yet have (open item in the handover: wheel slip and scale errors). When the sim gets them, back-EMF counting should be a separate, larger error than an encoder would give.
- **Needs from hardware:** an ADC channel per motor (the PIC32MX250 has 10-bit ADC channels; the count needed is the open pin-allocation question) or a bridge with a sense output.
- **Era fit:** the period's hobby and academic robots (Handy Board class) counted wheels with cheap sensors or inferred speed from motor current. Back-EMF is in that spirit.

## 3. Charger: TI BQ25185 (working, 2026-11-13)

- **What it is (verified from TI's listings):** BQ25185 1-cell, 1 A standalone linear battery charger with power path, factory mode and battery-tracking VINDPM; input 3.0 to 18 V (25 V tolerant); charge current 5 mA to 1 A set by the ISET pin; input current limit 100, 500 or 1100 mA set by the ILIM/VSET pin; system output up to 3.125 A, regulated at 4.5 V; VINDPM tracks the battery with a 330 mV offset when VBAT is above 3.5 V ([TI datasheet](https://focus.ti.com/document-viewer/bq25185/datasheet), [product page](https://ti.com/product/BQ25185)).
- **Cell wiring (my proposal):** put all nine cell parts of both lids in parallel, one rail of about 4.5 to 5 V, behind a diode per lid so that a shaded lid does not load a lit one. The SM531K08TFS (4.46 V) will pull the 5.02 V parts down slightly; accepted.
- **Why this part (my reasoning):** the input voltage regulation backs the charger off in dim light instead of collapsing the cell voltage, which is a crude maximum-power-point control; a lid is about 3 W in full sun (about 0.6 A at 5 V, my estimate from the datasheet figures) and tens of mW indoors, inside the 1 A limit; it needs no inductor.
- **Rejected:** BQ25570 (output at most about 110 mA, built for a single small cell; [datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/a/a/9/0/8/bq25570_Datasheet.pdf)); BQ24074 as the fallback (1.5 A; its input regulation threshold is not battery-tracking; [datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/gpn/bq24074)).
- **Not verified:** solar and MPPT behaviour in the full datasheet (SLUSF65), the package size (my "2 x 2 mm" was from memory), and the minimum useful input. A linear charger from 5 V to a 3.7 V battery wastes about a quarter of the power as heat (my arithmetic).

## 4. Battery sizing (`hardware/body/battery_sizing.py`, 2026-11-13)

**Correction to my earlier statement.** I said a 1S 500 mAh cell could not feed the servos because the STS3215 draws 2.7 A at 12 V, "about 9 A at the battery". That was the locked-rotor current, which the loads never need: the strut loads (172 mN.m to hold, 343 mN.m to push with a pair, `docs/drivetrain-spec.md`) are about 6 to 12% of the 2.94 N.m stall torque, so the servo current is about 0.16 to 0.32 A per servo (torque / 1.09 N.m per A, assuming linear torque and current, an assumption), and the battery supplies the winding loss, not V times that current. The firmware must still limit servo torque so that a jam cannot reach the stall current.

**Result (my model, assumed figures noted in the script):** battery-side load, before converter loss:

| Case | x1 load margin | x2 load margin (idle 30 mA) |
|---|---|---|
| Driving, struts holding (wheels, camera, controller, radio) | 1.6 to 4.2 W | 4.1 W |
| Posing one strut | 1.4 to 4.0 W | 4.1 W |
| Self-righting (one pair pushing, two holding) | 2.3 to 4.9 W | 7.5 W |

The range in the x1 column is the servo idle current, which the sources disagree on: 6 mA (Feetech datasheet, machine-translated; [servodatabase](https://servodatabase.com/servo/feetech/sts3215) 60 mA) or 30 mA ([DFRobot](https://www.dfrobot.com/product-2962.html)). **Four servos idling cost 0.3 W at 6 mA and 2.9 W at 60 mA, so the idle current, not the lifting, sets the running power.** It is the first thing to measure when hardware exists, and two firmware levers exist: torque off between moves (if the 345:1 gearing holds a pose unpowered, unverified) and a lower supply voltage.

**Options at the worst realistic case (self-right at x2 margin, idle 30 mA, 85% converters):**

| Option | Energy | Peak at the pack | C-rate | Driving hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1S 500 mAh, servos on 12 V boost | 1.85 Wh | 2.9 A | 5.9 C | 0.6 |
| 1S 1000 mAh, servos on 12 V boost | 3.70 Wh | 2.9 A | 2.9 C | 1.1 |
| 1S 1000 mAh, servos on 7.4 V boost | 3.70 Wh | 2.7 A | 2.7 C | 1.4 |
| 2S 500 mAh, servos straight off the pack | 3.70 Wh | 1.2 A | 2.4 C | 1.6 |

**Room (assumed 400 Wh/L pouch cell, from memory):** the placeholder bay (5 x 30 x 24 mm) holds about 1.4 Wh (390 mAh), too small; the whole 16 mm gap between the servo pairs (16 x 30 x 24 mm) holds about 4.6 Wh (1250 mAh at 3.7 V). The battery placeholder in `layout.py` should grow to that gap.

**Working recommendation (mine, awaiting the owner's word):**
- **1S, about 1000 mAh, 3C or better, filling the 16 mm gap**, in place of the 12 g, 500 mAh placeholder (about 20 g; to be settled when a real cell is chosen).
- **One boost converter to about 7.4 V for the servos and wheel drivers.** The STS3215's listed range is 4 to 14 V, so 12 V is not needed; at 7.4 V stall torque is about 1.8 N.m (my scaling, 2.6 times the largest need with margin) and speed about 60% of the 12 V figure (to be checked against the posing time).
- **A 3.3 V buck-boost for the logic** (a 1S pack falls to 3.0 V, too low for an LDO at 3.3 V).
- **The BQ25185 stays** as the charger (1S, 1 A), so the cell wiring in section 3 stands.
- **2S rejected for now:** it would halve the peak pack current and drop the servo boost, but the 5 V cell string cannot charge a 2S pack without a boost-charger, which is more parts than the one boost it saves.
- **Not checked:** the wheel motors' supply voltage (the docs give their no-load current at 6 V; they may run straight from the 1S pack or from the 7.4 V rail through PWM), and which 1S cell supplies 3C in this size.
**Picks (2026-11-13, working choices; figures are from TI's pages and retailer listings, not the full datasheets):**
- **Cell: Overlander SKU 2809, 1S 1000 mAh, 25C constant and 50C burst.** The 25C rating is far more than the 2.7 A (2.7C) worst case needs; the point is that a small cell of this kind exists at 1000 mAh with real discharge headroom. It lies in the 16 mm gap (10 x 51 x 31 mm box in `layout.py`, thickness along x), 28.5 g instead of the 12 g placeholder: **the body contents are now over the 386 g budget by 51 g** (was 35 g); the budget is deferred (owner). **Rejected:** the DNK602272 (74 x 22 x 6 mm, 16.7 g, 1000 mAh, with a protection circuit) because its sheet gives only 1C maximum continuous (1 A) and 3C for 10 ms, and its protection trips at 3 to 5 A ([DNK sheet](https://www.dnkpower.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/DNK602272-3.7V-1000mAh-Lipo-Battery-Specification.pdf)); our worst case is 2.7 A for self-righting and about 1.4 A at the unmargined load. A cell with a protection circuit and a higher rate is the thing to look for if a cell without protection is unwelcome. **Needed:** a protection circuit (over-discharge, over-current, short) on the power board; the cell is assumed to have none. Capacity check: 1.0 Ah at 3.7 V is 3.7 Wh, about 95 min of driving (`power_budget.py`, scenario D).
- **Servo and wheel rail: TPS61288** (TI, [product page](https://ti.com/product/TPS61288)): input 2.0 to 18 V with 2.4 V minimum start-up, output 4.5 to 18 V, 15 A peak switch current limit, 110 uA typical quiescent current into VOUT, 2.1 uA maximum shutdown current, PFM at light load, VQFN-HR about 3 x 2 mm (the page also says 2.5 x 3.0 mm), active. At 7.4 V and 1.2 A (8.9 W) the pack side carries about 3.5 A at 3.0 V and 85% (my arithmetic), inside the switch limit. **Not confirmed:** its load disconnect (the page does not mention one: with the converter off, the input still feeds the output through the inductor and diode path, so the servos need a load switch or the converter must be enabled only when moving, to be settled in the schematic) and its maximum output current. Rejected: TPS61022 and TPS61099 (output limited to 5.5 V; [TPS61022](https://ti.com/document-viewer/TPS61022/datasheet), [TPS61099](https://www.ti.com/product/tps61099)); TPS61093 (1.6 to 6 V input, up to 17 V output, but a small-current part and I did not confirm its current limit).
- **Logic rail: TPS63802** (TI, [datasheet](https://ti.com/lit/pdf/SLVSEU9)): 1.3 to 5.5 V input (1.8 V to start), 2 A at 3.3 V for inputs of 2.3 V and up, 11 uA quiescent current, 10-pin VSON-HR (3.0 x 2.0 mm by one page, 1.4 x 2.3 mm by another). A pack down to 3.0 V is inside its range. The 3.3 V load is about 0.3 to 0.45 A (controller, radio, camera, ToF; my sum), so 2 A is generous; **the alternative is the TPS63900** (400 mA at 3.3 V, 75 nA quiescent current, 2.5 x 2.5 mm; [page](https://www.ti.com.cn/lit/gpn/TPS63900)) if the keep-alive tier of `docs/body-redesign.md` wants a very-low-quiescent rail, with the 3.3 V loads that exceed 400 mA moved to a second converter.
- **Changed with the cell:** `power_budget.py` and the sim's `batteryWh` (1.85 to 3.70 Wh; 21 sim checks pass).

## 5. Sensors and controller details (2026-11-13)

**Ranging: ST VL53L5CX (working).** Read from the datasheet itself (DS13754 rev 2, August 2021, [PDF](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/6/e/3/0/6/vl53l5cx-datasheet.pdf)):
- 940 nm VCSEL, 8 x 8 or 4 x 4 zones, 63 degree diagonal square field of view, range 2 to 400 cm per zone, up to 60 Hz (15 Hz at 8 x 8, per the user manual), optical LGA16, 6.4 x 3.0 x 1.5 mm, -30 to 85 C.
- Supplies: AVDD 2.8 or 3.3 V, IOVDD 1.8, 2.8 or 3.3 V (so the 3.3 V logic rail serves both). I2C 400 kHz to 1 MHz, address 0x52; 47 kohm pull-up on the I2C reset and power-enable pins, 2.2 kohm on the clock line.
- **Current, active ranging (table 12): AVDD 45 mA typical (50 maximum) and IOVDD 50 mA typical (80 maximum); peaks are the average plus 10 mA each. Continuous ranging at 3.3 V is 313 mW (table 13).** That is 2.7 times the controller and radio together and is the biggest logic-side load. Autonomous mode at 8 x 8 and 1 Hz is 11 mW; the 5 Hz row of the table did not extract, so the sim's 5 Hz frame rate costs somewhere between 11 and 313 mW (not determined). The firmware (the ST ULD driver, which needs about 84 KB of firmware download at start-up per ST's manual: my memory, unchecked) is a load on the PIC32's 32 KB RAM and 128 KB flash to check.
- `power_budget.py` now carries the ranging load: scenario C 1.01 W, D 2.65 W, E 4.25 W; driving runtime on the 1000 mAh cell falls from 95 to 84 min.
- **Mounting (placed 2026-11-13, revised twice the same day at the owner's request; `layout.py`, `block.py`):** **behind the camera window, which stays a circle on the same centre (x = +48.07, z = 30) but grows: the through-wall opening and its one cover glass go from 26 to 36 mm across (radius 18 mm).** The camera's and lamp's view is still the 26 mm circle inside it. The sensor sits in the ring on the +x side of the beamsplitter plate (the plate spans x 38 to 58; the lamp comes from -x), at x = +62.07 and the camera-axis height z = 30, just behind the glass, looking +y; its board is 7 mm wide (x 58.6 to 65.6) and the opening's edge at that height is x = 66.07, so it fits by about 0.5 mm: **tight**. **Why +x and not up (my reasoning and the choice):** the same sensor needs a 36 mm circle at the side but a 39 mm one above the plate (the plate fills the circle up to z = 42), and the camera-axis height (62 mm above the ground) is already above the sim's assumed 52 mm (`sim/src/constants.ts`, `mountH`), so going higher bought nothing; the opening's rims are then 3.9 mm to the cell pocket and 3.9 mm to the face edge (equal, because the hole is centred between them). **Why not behind the plate itself:** the 50/50 plate passes half the light each way, so the sensor's emitted and returned light would be cut to a quarter, its surfaces add ghost reflections in the sensor's close range, and the camera module fills the plate's whole width behind it. The sim's 22 degree downward tilt would be a small wedge under the sensor board (not modelled). **Open:** the cover-glass material and thickness (the datasheet's histogram handles glass crosstalk beyond 60 cm; closer glass needs ST's cover-glass guide), the gap from sensor to glass (about 1 mm now; it should sit against the glass with a spacer), the thin 3.9 mm rims beside a 36 mm opening (a thin carbon edge to check), whether the camera sees the 940 nm light (an IR-cut filter would hide it), and the lamp's light at the sensor.

**Inertial: TDK ICM-42688-P (working).** [TDK page](https://invensense.tdk.com/products/icm-42688-p/): 2.8 mdps/rtHz gyro noise, 70 ug/rtHz accelerometer noise, 0.88 mA in low-noise 6-axis mode, 2.5 x 3 x 0.91 mm LGA-14; supply 1.71 to 3.6 V for both VDD and VDDIO, SPI to 24 MHz, I2C to 1 MHz, I3C to 12.5 MHz (secondary listings, datasheet DS-000347 not read in full). Gyro full scale of 2000 dps and accelerometer 16 g are listed. Runs from the 3.3 V rail; I2C or SPI both possible.

**Controller: PIC32MX250F128B pin budget (distributor listings, not the datasheet; to confirm).** 28-pin SPDIP or SOIC (21 I/O), two UARTs, two SPI, two I2C, peripheral pin select, 9 or 10 ADC channels (sources differ), five output-compare (PWM) channels by one listing. Proposed allocation: UART1 to the XBee (up to 250 kbit/s is well inside the 12.5 Mbps limit), UART2 to the servo bus (half duplex, 1 Mbaud, needs a direction control or a one-wire trick), I2C1 for the VL53L5CX and ICM-42688-P (two devices, different addresses), output-compare PWM for the four wheel drivers (a bridge per motor needs two inputs, so eight outputs, or a driver with a direction pin and PWM: four PWM plus four direction), ADC channels for the four back-EMF sensors plus battery voltage and cell current (six, within the nine or ten). That uses most of the 21 I/O; **it is tight and must be counted against the real pin table before the board is fixed.**

## 6. Wheel drivers, servo bus, operator radio (2026-11-13)

**Wheel motor, corrected: the Pololu #3068 is 248.98:1, not 256:1** ([Pololu specs](https://www.pololu.com/product/3068/specs), via search summaries): 6 V nominal, 130 rpm no-load (+-20%), 0.15 A no-load, 1.5 A stall, 3.2 kg.cm stall (0.31 N.m), 1.1 W maximum output power, 10 x 12 x 25 mm, 3 mm D shaft, no encoder. Any place in the docs that calls it 256:1 (the maxon GPX) is the earlier motor. At 65 mm wheels, 130 rpm would be 0.44 m/s; the 40 mm/s driving target is about 12 rpm, about 9% of no-load speed, so the motor runs at low duty. Stall is 4 ohm (6 V / 1.5 A); a 27 mN.m slope load is about 0.15 A above no-load and the 138 mN.m step about 0.7 A (linear torque and current, my assumption).

**Wheel driver: TI DRV8837, one per motor (working).** 1.8 A, motor supply 0 to 11 V by TI's page (1.8 to 11 V in an older datasheet), logic 1.8 to 7 V, IN1/IN2 control, 120 nA maximum sleep current by TI (distributor listings 35 nA typical), 8-pin WSON 2.0 x 2.0 mm ([TI page](https://www.ti.com/product/DRV8837/part-details/DRV8837DSGR); datasheet SLVSBA4F not read in full). Rejected: the DRV8838 (PH/EN control): its enable-low state is, as I recall, a brake, which cannot be used for back-EMF sensing (to confirm in the datasheet).
- **Why this suits back-EMF counting (my reasoning, from the remembered IN1/IN2 table: 0,0 high-impedance coast; 1,0 and 0,1 drive; 1,1 brake; to confirm in the datasheet):** driving forward as PWM on IN1 with IN2 low makes the off-time of every PWM period a coast gap, so the motor terminal floats and shows its back-EMF there, which the ADC samples through a divider. At the 12 rpm target the EMF is about 0.55 V (6 V x 12/130), against a 3.2 mV step on a 10-bit, 3.3 V ADC: 0.6% of that speed per count.
- **Rail:** the drivers sit on the 7.4 V servo rail, so the firmware must cap the average motor voltage at 6 V (about 81% duty). The motors are 6 V parts.
- **Reverse:** needs PWM on IN2 with IN1 low. The PIC32MX250 has five output-compare channels and PPS can remap an output pin at run time, so one channel per motor can be moved between IN1 and IN2 with the direction; this needs both pins of each driver in that channel's PPS output group (to check against the pin table).

**Servo bus interface (working, not a part number yet).** The STS3215 is a half-duplex UART bus, 8N1, 1 Mbps default, up to 253 servos on one line, TTL-level (search summaries; the logic voltage is not stated). The PIC32's UART runs to 12.5 Mbps (listing), so 1 Mbps is no problem. A bare TX-to-bus wiring is reported not to read back reliably (an ESP32 forum user could move the servo but not read it), so the interface needs a direction control: **a single tri-state buffer (such as a 74LVC1G125) driving the bus line from TX, enabled only while sending, with RX tied to the line** (my proposal; the search did not confirm that part; the Waveshare Bus Servo Driver HAT is a ready reference for the circuit). It costs one extra pin for the enable, or the enable can be driven from the UART's own TX-enable if the PIC32 UART offers it (to check). Servo pull-up and logic level need checking against the servo's documentation; the 3.3 V logic rail may need a level shifter if the servo's bus is 5 V.

**Operator-side radio (working).** A second XBee 3 802.15.4 on the console end, joined to the same PAN and channel. The USB adapter that Digi lists is the **XU3-A11** (XBee 3 USB adapter, 250 kbps, about 115 to 128 USD by distributor listings; the earlier XBIB-U-DEV is obsolete) ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.ph/en/products/detail/digi/XU3-A11/10292355)); a cheaper route is a bare XBee 3 on a USB-serial breakout (SparkFun lists an XBee 3 Thing Plus at 56.50 USD, [SparkFun](https://www.sparkfun.com/sparkfun-thing-plus-xbee3-micro-u-fl.html)). The console (browser, Web Serial) sees a serial port either way. Purchasing is deferred, so this is for the record.

**Pin budget, recounted with the above (finding): the 28-pin PIC32MX250F128B has 21 I/O and does not fit.** XBee UART 2, servo-bus UART 2 plus 1 direction, I2C 2, wheel drivers 8 (IN1 and IN2 for four motors) plus a shared nSLEEP, ADC 6 (four back-EMF, battery voltage, cell current), the XBee's sleep and reset lines (2): about 25, with programming pins on top. **Decision (mine, 2026-11-13, the owner trusts my judgement on this kind of call): move to the 44-pin PIC32MX250F128D in the same family.** Listings give 31 to 34 I/O (a family table 34, Farnell tables 33 and 31) and up to 13 analogue inputs ([listing](https://www.embeddedrelated.com/parts/p/PIC32MX250F128D-I/PT)); the pin need is about 25 plus programming, so even 31 fits with a few spare. It keeps the single-controller, same-family idea and the 32 KB RAM, and costs only package size (a TQFP-44 is 10 x 10 mm; the QFN-44 is 8 x 8 mm). Considered and rejected: multiplexing the ADC inputs through an analogue switch (an extra part and a slower sampling schedule for the back-EMF gaps). Still to confirm in the datasheet: the exact I/O count and the output-compare count.
