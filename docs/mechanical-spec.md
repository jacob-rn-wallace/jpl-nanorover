# Mechanical details: retention, couplings, seals, cables, stops, lids

Started 2026-11-13, as part of the push to specify the rover's own hardware (`docs/hardware-spec.md`). These are the items that `strut-spec.md`, `drivetrain-spec.md` and `body-redesign.md` list as "not modelled". Every choice here is **mine (recommended, owner has delegated this kind of call), from general engineering practice, not from a source**, and none is checked in CAD yet; sizes marked ASM are assumptions. Where the owner had already decided something, it is quoted.

## 1. Wheel to stub shaft

**Choice:** a D-flat on the 3 mm stub shaft and one M2 x 3 cup-point set screw through the wheel's apex collar onto the flat. The existing collar (`strut-spec.md`, apex boss and collar at |x| = 136.85) gets a tapped M2 radial hole. **Why:** a flat plus a set screw is the standard way to fix a hub to a 3 mm shaft; the wheel torque (at most 0.14 N.m at the wheel for the step case, `drivetrain-spec.md`) is well within what one M2 set screw on a flat holds (ASM, no figure checked). **Add:** a drop of thread-locker; the screw is the wheel's only axial retention, so the wheel also needs the bearing pair's inner faces to bear on a step (the existing 4 mm bearing span already does). **Change if:** the wheel is ever removed often (then a second screw, 90 degrees apart, on a second flat).

## 2. Spindle tube in the strut-tip bore

**Choice:** the 19 mm tube is a slip fit in the 19.1 mm bore, held by a **split clamp**: a slit through the tip's bore wall and one M2 screw across it, plus retaining compound in the bore as a backup. The tube's 1.5 mm outboard cap is its axial stop in one direction, the clamp holds the other. **Why:** `strut-spec.md` already calls for a clamp; a slit-and-screw clamp is the simplest, and the strut is also the motor's heat path, so a tight clamp helps. **Check done (2026-11-14, `hardware/strut/pivot_checks.py`, hand arithmetic with assumed material figures):** the tip wall round the 19.1 mm bore is 2.45 mm (24 mm tip), and the clamp is only the 3 mm plate thick. **Strength is not the problem:** the motor's stall reaction (0.31 N.m) needs a bore pressure of 0.4 to 0.9 MPa (a hoop stress in the tip wall of 1.4 to 3.6 MPa); the 6x bump load on the wheel (53 N, 41 mm from the clamp) puts a 2.2 N.m moment on the tube, which a 3 mm clamp resists with two edge forces of about 720 N, an edge pressure of about 38 MPa (6061-T6 yields at about 275 MPa, ASM); the tube itself sees 6.8 MPa in bending. **The weak point is stiffness:** over a 3 mm clamp length, a 0.1 mm slip fit tilts the tube by 1.9 degrees if it is not clamped. **So:** a tight fit (a few hundredths of a millimetre) plus retaining compound, not a slip fit and a screw; and **the M2 clamp screw needs an ear at the slit**, since a 2.45 mm wall cannot carry a screw head and thread (the plate is 3 mm thick, so an ear in the plate's plane would hold an M2 thread with about 1.5 diameters of engagement). **If the wheel wobbles in the build:** a longer clamp would need a thicker tip, which strut B cannot take inboard (the 1 mm gap to strut A's plate) or outboard (the wheel's end plate); a bolted flange on the tube (three M2 screws on a 22 mm circle) is the alternative, with a larger tip boss (about 28 mm).

## 3. Motor-to-stub coupling

**Choice:** a single aluminium sleeve, 3 mm bore each end, 7.5 mm long (the length the CAD has room for, `drivetrain-spec.md` calls it tight), with one M2 set screw per end, the motor side onto the Pololu's D-flat and the stub side onto a flat. **Why:** it needs no part bought beyond a rod of aluminium, and the load is small. **Fallback if the screws do not fit in 7.5 mm:** bond the sleeve to the stub with retaining compound and use one set screw on the motor side only. **Note:** the Pololu #3068's output is a 3 mm D-shaft (`hardware-spec.md`, section 6), so the motor side is a standard D-flat.

## 4. Dust and seals

**Choice: shielded ball bearings (the "ZZ" type) and a labyrinth step, no rubber seals.** The wheel's collar overlaps the tube's cap by about 1 mm with a 0.2 mm gap (ASM), and the same at the hub housing. **Why:** a rubber lip seal adds friction that the 0.15 N.m wheel budget and the 0.17 N.m strut hold do not want, and the use case is an indoor tabletop (`docs/sim-spec.md`) where dust is not a function requirement. **Not covered:** fine dust that works through a labyrinth over hours; if the rover ever runs on regolith-like material, revisit with lip seals and a stronger servo.

## 5. Hub: adapters and pinions fixed to the servo horns

**Choice:** each horn adapter and pinion is a flanged part **bolted to the servo's supplied metal horn through the horn's own screw holes** (M2, ASM: the horn's pattern and thread are not confirmed), with the horn on the servo's 25-tooth output spline and its centre screw. **Why:** it uses the servo's own spline, so no shaft is broached and the servo output keeps its own backlash rating. **To do:** read the real horn dimensions from the Feetech drawing before the adapter diameters are fixed (the 20 mm horn adapter and the 28 mm housing flange in `hub.py` are placeholders); gear teeth at the 1:1 pair are still plain discs at the pitch circle.

## 6. Wire passage across the strut pivots

Each strut carries one wheel motor, so **two wires per strut, eight across the four pivots**, and the inner strut shaft (5 mm steel) is solid, so a wire cannot run through it.
**Choice:** a **limited-rotation strut with a service loop**: a flat or silicone-insulated two-conductor cable looped around the strut's hub boss, entering the body through a hole in the wall beside the hub's spot-face with a printed or moulded strain-relief and a dab of sealant. **Why:** slip rings at 19 mm are bulky and the wire count is only 8. **Corrected on 2026-11-14 (the first version said about plus or minus 170 degrees and claimed that the chassis simulation's poses stay within a half turn: that claim was not supported and was wrong):** the chassis simulation (`hardware/drivetrain/chassis_sim.py`) allows each strut any angle over the full circle, subject only to the two struts staying at least 60 degrees apart; the self-righting search (`hardware/strut/pivot_checks.py`, which runs it) moves strut A by up to **195 degrees** and strut B by **180 degrees** from the start stance in a single sequence. So the strut travel must be at least about 200 degrees one way and about 150 the other from its home stance, not 170 either way; see item 7. **Cable numbers (my arithmetic, `pivot_checks.py`):** wrapping or unwrapping the cable on the 30 mm boss (cable centre at radius 16.5 mm) takes 98 mm of cable for 340 degrees of travel, 104 mm for 360, 156 mm for 540; the free run along the arm to the tip is 55 mm; five turns of a 1.3 mm pair fit along the boss's 7 mm, but the 1 mm gap to the neighbouring strut limits a pair to one layer. **Open:** the second strut's wires (strut B's plate is behind strut A's plate, with only the 1 mm gap between them: they run in a groove in the inboard face of B's plate) and the path through the wall near the hub. **Check in CAD:** the loop's clearance to the wheel and the hub housing (`hardware/hub/hub.py`), and wire fatigue at 170 degrees (ASM: silicone-insulated multistrand is fine for the cycle count of a hobby rover).

## 7. Strut stops

**Choice (revised 2026-11-14):** a hard stop that limits each strut to **about 340 degrees of travel, placed asymmetrically about its home stance** (home: A at -30 degrees and B at 210, `chassis_sim.py`): from about 145 degrees one way to about 195 the other, which holds the self-righting search's single solution (strut A moving 0 to +195, B moving -15 to +150 for the lowest peak torque; A -150 to 0 and B -180 to 0 for the fewest moves), and a **20 degree dead sector about opposite the home stance** (at about 205 degrees from home). Firmware soft limits sit 5 degrees inside the stops (the STS3215 has angle-limit registers, from my memory of the protocol, to confirm in the manual). **Why:** a pin in the housing and a tab on the strut hub can limit travel to at most 360 degrees minus the tab and pin widths, so 340 degrees is what a simple stop gives; a stop that allows more than a turn needs a free-running ring between pin and tab, which is more parts. **What I do not know:** the self-righting search starts only from one inverted pose; righting from the other rest orientations (the simulation found 13,836 stable states) may need other strut angles, so the dead sector might block one of them. **The check to run before the stops are fixed:** the self-righting search from every rest orientation, with each strut's excursion limited to the 340 degree window; if some orientations fail, widen the travel with a free-ring stop, or shorten the dead sector. **Why a hard stop at all:** a servo at 0.17 N.m on a 16.5 mm boss pulls a taut wire with about 10 N, about the breaking load of a 30 AWG strand (ASM), so the servo's own torque limit is not a safe protection.

## 8. The lids and their screws: a finding, and the recommendation

**Finding:** each lid is almost all cell. The cell pocket is about 134.4 x 133.9 mm in a 140 x 140 mm face (`body-redesign.md`), leaving a **rim of only 2.8 mm (sides) and 3.05 mm (top and bottom)**. An M2 pan head is about 3.8 mm across (ASM), so screws **cannot go through the lid face**, and the rim cannot carry a gasket and a screw boss as well.
**Owner's standing decision (2026-11-12, `body-redesign.md`):** for now the lids are not separate components in the design process; the body is one block of forged carbon, and the lid joint, lid screws and board-to-board link wait until the body is more concrete.
**Recommendation (mine) when the lids come back:** retain each lid by **side-entry screws**: M2 screws going in horizontally through the body wall's outer rim into threaded inserts in the lid's edge, on the two faces that have room (the hub faces have the hub hardware in the middle but free corners; the end faces have the window and the cell pocket, so the hub faces are the better choice), eight per lid or so (ASM, to match the gasket path of about 520 mm); **seal with a flat die-cut silicone gasket, 0.5 mm (ASM), on a flange inside the rim**, dust-tight rather than watertight, to match the tabletop use case. **Alternative:** a bonded lid (not removable), which loses the cell-replacement plan the owner asked for (`body-redesign.md`, item 3 of the cell decisions). I do not recommend it.
**Also needed for the removable lid:** a board-to-board connector at the joint (`body-redesign.md`, "Two lids"), and the cells' wiring through it.
