# Mechanical details: retention, couplings, seals, cables, stops, lids

Started 2026-11-13, as part of the push to specify the rover's own hardware (`docs/hardware-spec.md`). These are the items that `strut-spec.md`, `drivetrain-spec.md` and `body-redesign.md` list as "not modelled". Every choice here is **mine (recommended, owner has delegated this kind of call), from general engineering practice, not from a source**, and none is checked in CAD yet; sizes marked ASM are assumptions. Where the owner had already decided something, it is quoted.

## 1. Wheel to stub shaft

**Choice:** a D-flat on the 3 mm stub shaft and one M2 x 3 cup-point set screw through the wheel's apex collar onto the flat. The existing collar (`strut-spec.md`, apex boss and collar at |x| = 136.85) gets a tapped M2 radial hole. **Why:** a flat plus a set screw is the standard way to fix a hub to a 3 mm shaft; the wheel torque (at most 0.14 N.m at the wheel for the step case, `drivetrain-spec.md`) is well within what one M2 set screw on a flat holds (ASM, no figure checked). **Add:** a drop of thread-locker; the screw is the wheel's only axial retention, so the wheel also needs the bearing pair's inner faces to bear on a step (the existing 4 mm bearing span already does). **Change if:** the wheel is ever removed often (then a second screw, 90 degrees apart, on a second flat).

## 2. Spindle tube in the strut-tip bore

**Choice:** the 19 mm tube is a slip fit in the 19.1 mm bore, held by a **split clamp**: a slit through the tip's bore wall and one M2 screw across it, plus retaining compound in the bore as a backup. The tube's 1.5 mm outboard cap is its axial stop in one direction, the clamp holds the other. **Why:** `strut-spec.md` already calls for a clamp; a slit-and-screw clamp is the simplest, and the strut is also the motor's heat path, so a tight clamp helps. **Check done (2026-11-14, `hardware/strut/pivot_checks.py`, hand arithmetic with assumed material figures):** the tip wall round the 19.1 mm bore is 2.45 mm (24 mm tip), and the clamp is only the 3 mm plate thick. **Strength is not the problem:** the motor's stall reaction (0.31 N.m) needs a bore pressure of 0.4 to 0.9 MPa (a hoop stress in the tip wall of 1.4 to 3.6 MPa); the 6x bump load on the wheel (53 N, 41 mm from the clamp) puts a 2.2 N.m moment on the tube, which a 3 mm clamp resists with two edge forces of about 720 N, an edge pressure of about 38 MPa (6061-T6 yields at about 275 MPa, ASM); the tube itself sees 6.8 MPa in bending. **The weak point is stiffness:** over a 3 mm clamp length, a 0.1 mm slip fit tilts the tube by 1.9 degrees if it is not clamped. **So:** a tight fit (a few hundredths of a millimetre) plus retaining compound, not a slip fit and a screw; and **the M2 clamp screw needs an ear at the slit**, since a 2.45 mm wall cannot carry a screw head and thread (the plate is 3 mm thick, so an ear in the plate's plane would hold an M2 thread with about 1.5 diameters of engagement). **Done in CAD (2026-11-14, `hardware/strut/strut.py`; `COLLAR_IN`, `SLIT_W`, `SCREW_X`):** each strut's tip boss gets a **4 mm collar on its inboard side** (toward the body wall at |x| = 70; the collar's inboard face is at |x| = 73.25 for strut A and 77.25 for B, 3.25 mm clear of the wall for A), making the clamp **7 mm long** instead of 3; the spindle tube starts as far inboard (the motor stays where it was, so the extra 4 mm of tube is empty sleeve, and on strut B the collar surrounds the motor's last 4 mm); a **0.6 mm slit** runs from the bore through the tip's outer wall along the arm; and a **2.0 mm clamp-screw hole with a 3.9 mm counterbore crosses the slit** at 10.8 mm from the tip centre, at the middle of the plate and collar (7 mm of material, so the head and thread fit: the earlier 3 mm plate could not). **Effect (`pivot_checks.py`):** the bore edge force in a 6x bump falls from 724 N to 326 N (edge pressure 38 to 17 MPa) and the unclamped tilt from 1.9 to 0.8 degrees. **Checked:** `strut.py` finds interference between strut A and B only at the same 12 equal-angle pairs as before (the two struts pointing the same way, which the 60 degree rule excludes anyway), and 0.00 mm3 against the body, the hub, the wheels and the spindle at the default pose. **Not checked:** the collar against the body at every strut angle (only the default pose); the real clearance to the carbon body, whose side wall is at 70 mm, not Hammond's; and the retaining compound's cure space, since a bonded tight fit is the plan.
**If the wheel wobbles in the build:** a longer clamp would need a thicker tip, which strut B cannot take inboard (the 1 mm gap to strut A's plate) or outboard (the wheel's end plate); a bolted flange on the tube (three M2 screws on a 22 mm circle) is the alternative, with a larger tip boss (about 28 mm).

## 3. Motor-to-stub coupling

**Choice:** a single aluminium sleeve, 3 mm bore each end, 7.5 mm long (the length the CAD has room for, `drivetrain-spec.md` calls it tight), with one M2 set screw per end, the motor side onto the Pololu's D-flat and the stub side onto a flat. **Why:** it needs no part bought beyond a rod of aluminium, and the load is small. **Fallback if the screws do not fit in 7.5 mm:** bond the sleeve to the stub with retaining compound and use one set screw on the motor side only. **Note:** the Pololu #3068's output is a 3 mm D-shaft (`hardware-spec.md`, section 6), so the motor side is a standard D-flat.

## 4. Dust and seals

**Choice: shielded ball bearings (the "ZZ" type) and a labyrinth step, no rubber seals.** The wheel's collar overlaps the tube's cap by about 1 mm with a 0.2 mm gap (ASM), and the same at the hub housing. **Why:** a rubber lip seal adds friction that the 0.15 N.m wheel budget and the 0.17 N.m strut hold do not want, and the use case is an indoor tabletop (`docs/sim-spec.md`) where dust is not a function requirement. **Not covered:** fine dust that works through a labyrinth over hours; if the rover ever runs on regolith-like material, revisit with lip seals and a stronger servo.

## 5. Hub: adapters and pinions fixed to the servo horns

**Choice:** each horn adapter and pinion is a flanged part **bolted to the servo's supplied metal horn through the horn's own screw holes** (M2, ASM: the horn's pattern and thread are not confirmed), with the horn on the servo's 25-tooth output spline and its centre screw. **Why:** it uses the servo's own spline, so no shaft is broached and the servo output keeps its own backlash rating. **To do:** read the real horn dimensions from the Feetech drawing before the adapter diameters are fixed (the 20 mm horn adapter and the 28 mm housing flange in `hub.py` are placeholders); gear teeth at the 1:1 pair are still plain discs at the pitch circle.

## 6. Wire passage across the strut pivots

Each strut carries one wheel motor, so **two wires per strut, eight across the four pivots**, and the inner strut shaft (5 mm steel) is solid, so a wire cannot run through it.
**Choice:** a **limited-rotation strut with a service loop**: a flat or silicone-insulated two-conductor cable looped around the strut's hub boss, entering the body through a hole in the wall beside the hub's spot-face with a printed or moulded strain-relief and a dab of sealant. **Why:** slip rings at 19 mm are bulky and the wire count is only 8. **Corrected on 2026-11-14 (the first version said about plus or minus 170 degrees and claimed that the chassis simulation's poses stay within a half turn: that claim was not supported and was wrong):** the chassis simulation (`hardware/drivetrain/chassis_sim.py`) allows each strut any angle over the full circle, subject only to the two struts staying at least 60 degrees apart; the self-righting search (`hardware/strut/pivot_checks.py`, which runs it) moves strut A by up to **195 degrees** and strut B by **180 degrees** from the start stance in a single sequence. So the strut travel must be at least about 195 degrees one way and 145 the other from its home stance, not 170 either way, and the dead sector has to be placed opposite home (item 7 has the check that shows why). **Cable numbers (my arithmetic, `pivot_checks.py`):** wrapping or unwrapping the cable on the 30 mm boss (cable centre at radius 16.5 mm) takes 98 mm of cable for 340 degrees of travel, 104 mm for 360, 156 mm for 540; the free run along the arm to the tip is 55 mm; five turns of a 1.3 mm pair fit along the boss's 7 mm, but the 1 mm gap to the neighbouring strut limits a pair to one layer. **Routing, worked through 2026-11-14 (my reasoning from `strut.py` and `hub.py`; not yet in CAD):** **strut A's wires** leave the motor tail (flush with A's plate inboard face, |x| = 77.25), run along the arm's inboard face (the plate is flush with the outer face of the boss, so a 4 mm channel lies between it and the body wall), and enter a **ring-shaped space of about 3 to 4 mm height around the boss (radius 15 to 19 mm), between the boss's inboard face at 73.25 and the wall at about 70**, where the slack is held as a spiral of about one turn (107 mm per turn at radius 17 mm; the 340 degree travel needs 98 mm) before a **2 mm hole through the wall at about 19 mm from the hub axis**, outside the 30 mm spot-face. **Strut B's wires cannot go that way:** B's inboard side faces strut A's boss and plate with only the 1 mm gap, and any route round A's boss crosses A's arm, which sweeps through 340 degrees, so A would pinch the wires. **So B's wires go through the strut B inner shaft:** make it hollow (5 mm outside, 3 mm bore; the bending stiffness stays 87% and the shear stress at the servo's 7.4 V stall torque, about 1.8 N.m, is about 84 MPa, against several hundred MPa for steel, my arithmetic), run the two wires from the tip through a groove in B's plate to the shaft's outboard end and in through a cross hole, and out of the shaft's inboard end in the body, where the twist over about 30 mm of free wire must stay within the 340 degree travel (a twisted silicone wire pair is routine at plus or minus 180 degrees over 30 mm, my judgement; it is the part to prove on a bench). **Open:** the actual CAD of the ring spiral, the cross hole and groove, the wall hole in the carbon body, and the wire's exit at servo B's horn adapter (flange 20 x 3 mm at |x| 48.5 to 51.5). **Check in CAD:** the loop's clearance to the wheel and the hub housing (`hardware/hub/hub.py`), and wire fatigue at 170 degrees (ASM: silicone-insulated multistrand is fine for the cycle count of a hobby rover).

## 7. Strut stops

**Checked 2026-11-14 with `hardware/strut/stop_window_check.py`** (it searches the chassis simulation's state graph, `hardware/drivetrain/chassis_sim.py`: strut angles on a 15 degree grid, one strut moving at a time, at least 60 degrees between the struts, the body settling by rolling downhill, from every rest state to an upright rest). With full-circle strut travel **every one of the 1,494 inverted rest states can right itself**. With limited travel (a hard stop), whether it works depends on where the dead sector is and on where the struts start:

| Window about each strut's home stance (A at 330 degrees, B at 210) | Stuck, starts anywhere in the window | Stuck, starts within +-45 / +-90 / +-120 degrees of home |
|---|---|---|
| **340 degrees, from -145 to +195 (asymmetric)** | 14.0% | **0 / 0.7% (3 of 458) / 6.3%** |
| 340 degrees, from -170 to +170 (symmetric) | 49.6% | 70.6% / 53.7% / 50.6% |
| 345 degrees, gap at six other places | 13 to 15% (one place: 49.7%) | not scored |
| 405 degrees, from -195 to +210 | 16.2% | 0 / 0.7% / 5.9% |
| 450 degrees, from -225 to +225 | 16.8% | 0 / 0.7% / 5.6% |
| 540 degrees, from -270 to +270 | 20.0% | not scored |

**What this says (my reading):** (1) **the symmetric plus or minus 170 degrees I first proposed would have stranded half the inverted poses: it is the worst placement tested**; the dead sector must sit roughly opposite the home stance, where the struts rarely go, and the other strut blocks it from the usual side; (2) **more travel than 340 degrees buys nothing** (405 and 450 degrees give the same 0.7% within 90 degrees of home): a strut at a window end needs to go further the same way, and the other strut, which it cannot pass, blocks the long way round; (3) the failures grow with distance from home, so the result depends on where the struts are when the rover is flipped.
**Choice:** a hard stop limiting each strut to **340 degrees, from 145 degrees on one side of its home stance to 195 on the other** (about the same dead sector, 20 degrees, opposite home), with firmware soft limits 5 degrees inside. **Plus a firmware rule: whenever the rover is upright and idle, return both struts to within about 90 degrees of home** (the working stances, `chassis-simulation.md`, keep A at 0 to -85 degrees and B at 180 to 265, which is 30 to -55 degrees from home for A and -30 to +55 for B, so this costs nothing in use). Then a flip while in a working pose leaves 0 to 0.7% of the starts stuck; a flip while the struts are 120 degrees from home leaves about 6%.
**What is not covered:** a rover flipped while its struts are more than 90 degrees from home (during a righting attempt, or posing), and the 3 stuck states within 90 degrees; for those the owner can accept a small risk, move to a free-ring stop (travel of more than a turn, more parts, which this search says does not help), or add a firmware recovery that moves a stranded strut the long way round when the other strut is out of the way. **Why a hard stop at all:** a servo at 0.17 N.m on a 16.5 mm boss pulls a taut wire with about 10 N, about the breaking load of a 30 AWG strand (ASM), so the servo's own torque limit is not a safe protection. A pin in the housing and a tab on the strut hub limit travel to 360 degrees minus their widths, so 340 degrees is what the simple stop gives.

## 8. The lids and their screws: a finding, and the recommendation

**Finding:** each lid is almost all cell. The cell pocket is about 134.4 x 133.9 mm in a 140 x 140 mm face (`body-redesign.md`), leaving a **rim of only 2.8 mm (sides) and 3.05 mm (top and bottom)**. An M2 pan head is about 3.8 mm across (ASM), so screws **cannot go through the lid face**, and the rim cannot carry a gasket and a screw boss as well.
**Owner's standing decision (2026-11-12, `body-redesign.md`):** for now the lids are not separate components in the design process; the body is one block of forged carbon, and the lid joint, lid screws and board-to-board link wait until the body is more concrete.
**Recommendation (mine) when the lids come back:** retain each lid by **side-entry screws**: M2 screws going in horizontally through the body wall's outer rim into threaded inserts in the lid's edge, on the two faces that have room (the hub faces have the hub hardware in the middle but free corners; the end faces have the window and the cell pocket, so the hub faces are the better choice), eight per lid or so (ASM, to match the gasket path of about 520 mm); **seal with a flat die-cut silicone gasket, 0.5 mm (ASM), on a flange inside the rim**, dust-tight rather than watertight, to match the tabletop use case. **Alternative:** a bonded lid (not removable), which loses the cell-replacement plan the owner asked for (`body-redesign.md`, item 3 of the cell decisions). I do not recommend it.
**Also needed for the removable lid:** a board-to-board connector at the joint (`body-redesign.md`, "Two lids"), and the cells' wiring through it.
