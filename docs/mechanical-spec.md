# Mechanical details: retention, couplings, seals, cables, stops, lids

Started 2026-11-13, as part of the push to specify the rover's own hardware (`docs/hardware-spec.md`). These are the items that `strut-spec.md`, `drivetrain-spec.md` and `body-redesign.md` list as "not modelled". Every choice here is **mine (recommended, owner has delegated this kind of call), from general engineering practice, not from a source**, and none is checked in CAD yet; sizes marked ASM are assumptions. Where the owner had already decided something, it is quoted.

## 1. Wheel to stub shaft

**Choice:** a D-flat on the 3 mm stub shaft and one M2 x 3 cup-point set screw through the wheel's apex collar onto the flat. The existing collar (`strut-spec.md`, apex boss and collar at |x| = 136.85) gets a tapped M2 radial hole. **Why:** a flat plus a set screw is the standard way to fix a hub to a 3 mm shaft; the wheel torque (at most 0.14 N.m at the wheel for the step case, `drivetrain-spec.md`) is well within what one M2 set screw on a flat holds (ASM, no figure checked). **Add:** a drop of thread-locker; the screw is the wheel's only axial retention, so the wheel also needs the bearing pair's inner faces to bear on a step (the existing 4 mm bearing span already does). **Change if:** the wheel is ever removed often (then a second screw, 90 degrees apart, on a second flat).

## 2. Spindle tube in the strut-tip bore

**Choice:** the 19 mm tube is a slip fit in the 19.1 mm bore, held by a **split clamp**: a slit through the tip's bore wall and one M2 screw across it, plus retaining compound in the bore as a backup. The tube's 1.5 mm outboard cap is its axial stop in one direction, the clamp holds the other. **Why:** `strut-spec.md` already calls for a clamp; a slit-and-screw clamp is the simplest, and the strut is also the motor's heat path, so a tight clamp helps. **Check done (2026-11-14, `hardware/strut/pivot_checks.py`, hand arithmetic with assumed material figures):** the tip wall round the 19.1 mm bore is 2.45 mm (24 mm tip), and the clamp is only the 3 mm plate thick. **Strength is not the problem:** the motor's stall reaction (0.31 N.m) needs a bore pressure of 0.4 to 0.9 MPa (a hoop stress in the tip wall of 1.4 to 3.6 MPa); the 6x bump load on the wheel (53 N, 41 mm from the clamp) puts a 2.2 N.m moment on the tube, which a 3 mm clamp resists with two edge forces of about 720 N, an edge pressure of about 38 MPa (6061-T6 yields at about 275 MPa, ASM); the tube itself sees 6.8 MPa in bending. **The weak point is stiffness:** over a 3 mm clamp length, a 0.1 mm slip fit tilts the tube by 1.9 degrees if it is not clamped. **So:** a tight fit (a few hundredths of a millimetre) plus retaining compound, not a slip fit and a screw; and **the M2 clamp screw needs an ear at the slit**, since a 2.45 mm wall cannot carry a screw head and thread (the plate is 3 mm thick, so an ear in the plate's plane would hold an M2 thread with about 1.5 diameters of engagement). **Done in CAD (2026-11-14, `hardware/strut/strut.py`; `COLLAR_IN`, `SLIT_W`, `SCREW_X`):** each strut's tip boss gets a **4 mm collar on its inboard side** (toward the body wall at |x| = 70; the collar's inboard face is at |x| = 73.25 for strut A and 77.25 for B, 3.25 mm clear of the wall for A), making the clamp **7 mm long** instead of 3; the spindle tube starts as far inboard (the motor stays where it was, so the extra 4 mm of tube is empty sleeve, and on strut B the collar surrounds the motor's last 4 mm); a **0.6 mm slit** runs from the bore through the tip's outer wall along the arm; and a **2.0 mm clamp-screw hole with a 3.9 mm counterbore crosses the slit** at 10.8 mm from the tip centre, at the middle of the plate and collar (7 mm of material, so the head and thread fit: the earlier 3 mm plate could not). **Effect (`pivot_checks.py`):** the bore edge force in a 6x bump falls from 724 N to 326 N (edge pressure 38 to 17 MPa) and the unclamped tilt from 1.9 to 0.8 degrees. **Checked:** `strut.py` finds interference between strut A and B only at the same 12 equal-angle pairs as before (the two struts pointing the same way, which the 60 degree rule excludes anyway), and 0.00 mm3 against the body, the hub, the wheels and the spindle at the default pose. **Not checked:** the collar against the body at every strut angle (only the default pose); the real clearance to the carbon body, whose side wall is at 70 mm, not Hammond's; and the retaining compound's cure space, since a bonded tight fit is the plan.
**If the wheel wobbles in the build:** a longer clamp would need a thicker tip, which strut B cannot take inboard (the 1 mm gap to strut A's plate) or outboard (the wheel's end plate); a bolted flange on the tube (three M2 screws on a 22 mm circle) is the alternative, with a larger tip boss (about 28 mm).

## 3. Motor-to-stub coupling

**Choice:** a single aluminium sleeve, 3 mm bore each end, 7.5 mm long (the length the CAD has room for, `drivetrain-spec.md` calls it tight), with one M2 set screw per end, the motor side onto the Pololu's D-flat and the stub side onto a flat. **Why:** it needs no part bought beyond a rod of aluminium, and the load is small. **Fallback if the screws do not fit in 7.5 mm:** bond the sleeve to the stub with retaining compound and use one set screw on the motor side only. **Note:** the Pololu #3068's output is a 3 mm D-shaft (`hardware-spec.md`, section 6), so the motor side is a standard D-flat.

## 4. Dust and seals

**Choice: shielded ball bearings (the "ZZ" type) and a labyrinth step, no rubber seals.** The wheel's collar overlaps the tube's cap by about 1 mm with a 0.2 mm gap (ASM), and the same at the hub housing. **Why:** a rubber lip seal adds friction that the 0.15 N.m wheel budget and the 0.17 N.m strut hold do not want, and the use case is an indoor tabletop (`docs/sim-spec.md`) where dust is not a function requirement. **Not covered:** fine dust that works through a labyrinth over hours; if the rover ever runs on regolith-like material, revisit with lip seals and a stronger servo.

## 5. Hub: adapters and pinions fixed to the servo horns

**Choice:** each horn adapter and pinion is a flanged part **bolted to the servo's supplied metal horn through the horn's own screw holes** (M2, ASM: the horn's pattern and thread are not confirmed), with the horn on the servo's 25-tooth output spline and its centre screw. **Why:** it uses the servo's own spline, so no shaft is broached and the servo output keeps its own backlash rating. **To do:** read the real horn dimensions from the Feetech drawing before the adapter diameters are fixed (the 20 mm horn adapter and the 28 mm housing flange in `hub.py` are placeholders); gear teeth at the 1:1 pair are still plain discs at the pitch circle.

## 6. Wire passage across the strut pivots

Each strut carries one wheel motor, so **two wires per strut, eight across the four pivots**, and the inner strut shaft (5 mm steel) is solid, so a wire cannot run through it.
**Choice:** a **limited-rotation strut with a service loop**: a flat or silicone-insulated two-conductor cable looped around the strut's hub boss, entering the body through a hole in the wall beside the hub's spot-face with a printed or moulded strain-relief and a dab of sealant. **Why:** slip rings at 19 mm are bulky and the wire count is only 8. **Corrected on 2026-11-14 (the first version said about plus or minus 170 degrees and claimed that the chassis simulation's poses stay within a half turn: that claim was not supported and was wrong):** the chassis simulation (`hardware/drivetrain/chassis_sim.py`) allows each strut any angle over the full circle, subject only to the two struts staying at least 60 degrees apart; the self-righting search (`hardware/strut/pivot_checks.py`, which runs it) moves strut A by up to **195 degrees** and strut B by **180 degrees** from the start stance in a single sequence. So the strut travel must be nearly a full turn: item 7 now finds that a stop leaving a dead sector of 10 degrees or so, placed at the angles given there (strut A between absolute 15 and 30 degrees, strut B between 345 and 360), strands none of the inverted rest states, so the travel is about 345 degrees and the cable wrap about 100 mm. **Cable numbers (my arithmetic, `pivot_checks.py`):** wrapping or unwrapping the cable on the 30 mm boss (cable centre at radius 16.5 mm) takes 98 mm of cable for 340 degrees of travel, 104 mm for 360, 156 mm for 540; the free run along the arm to the tip is 55 mm; five turns of a 1.3 mm pair fit along the boss's 7 mm, but the 1 mm gap to the neighbouring strut limits a pair to one layer. **Routing, worked through 2026-11-14 (my reasoning from `strut.py` and `hub.py`; not yet in CAD):** **strut A's wires** leave the motor tail (flush with A's plate inboard face, |x| = 77.25), run along the arm's inboard face (the plate is flush with the outer face of the boss, so a 4 mm channel lies between it and the body wall), and enter a **ring-shaped space of about 3 to 4 mm height around the boss (radius 15 to 19 mm), between the boss's inboard face at 73.25 and the wall at about 70**, where the slack is held as a spiral of about one turn (107 mm per turn at radius 17 mm; the 340 degree travel needs 98 mm) before a **2 mm hole through the wall at about 19 mm from the hub axis**, outside the 30 mm spot-face. **Strut B's wires cannot go that way:** B's inboard side faces strut A's boss and plate with only the 1 mm gap, and any route round A's boss crosses A's arm, which sweeps through 340 degrees, so A would pinch the wires. **So B's wires go through the strut B inner shaft:** make it hollow (5 mm outside, 3 mm bore; the bending stiffness stays 87% and the shear stress at the servo's 7.4 V stall torque, about 1.8 N.m, is about 84 MPa, against several hundred MPa for steel, my arithmetic), run the two wires from the tip through a groove in B's plate to the shaft's outboard end and in through a cross hole, and out of the shaft's inboard end in the body, where the twist over about 30 mm of free wire must stay within the 340 degree travel (a twisted silicone wire pair is routine at plus or minus 180 degrees over 30 mm, my judgement; it is the part to prove on a bench). **Done in CAD (2026-11-14):** `hardware/hub/hub.py` now makes the inner shaft hollow (`INNER_BORE` 3.0 mm, 4.0 g each) with a **1.2 mm cross hole at |x| = 52.25**, in the 1.5 mm window between servo B's horn adapter (ends at 51.5) and the hollow shaft and gear plane (starts at 53.0), so the wires leave the shaft there and pass out between the adapter flange and gear A (the hub's parts against each other: 0 overlapping pairs of 45); `hardware/body/block.py` cuts a **2 mm feedthrough hole through each side wall, 19 mm above the hub axis** (y = 0, z = 49, |x| 60 to 70; the block still re-imports as one solid and every placement is clear of it). **Still open:** the groove in B's plate from the tip to the shaft's outboard end and the cross hole there (the shaft's outboard end sticks out 4 mm beyond B's boss; the wires enter the bore at that end), the ring spiral (free space, not modelled), the wires' route from the wall holes to the power board, and the bench proof of the twist. **Check in CAD:** the loop's clearance to the wheel and the hub housing (`hardware/hub/hub.py`), and wire fatigue at 170 degrees (ASM: silicone-insulated multistrand is fine for the cycle count of a hobby rover).

## 7. Strut stops

**Superseded on 2026-11-14 (later the same day): a one-step dead gap, placed right, strands nothing.** Two searches of the chassis simulation's state graph (`hardware/drivetrain/chassis_sim.py`: strut angles on a 15 degree grid, one strut moving at a time, at least 60 degrees between the struts, the body settling downhill, from every rest state to an upright rest) decided this:

1. `hardware/strut/stop_window_check.py` (windows about home, starts anywhere or near home): full-circle travel strands none of the 1,494 inverted rest states; **the 340 degree window I first recommended (from -145 to +195 about home, a 30 degree dead sector) strands 3 of 458 starts within 90 degrees of home, 51 of 810 within 120, and 280 of 1,494 anywhere**; the symmetric plus or minus 170 degrees strands about half; more travel (405, 450, 540 degrees) does not help, because the struts cannot pass each other.
2. `hardware/strut/stop_gap_search.py` (builds the physical graph once, then tries every placement of the dead gap for each strut, 24 x 24, for gaps of one and two grid steps): **a one-step (15 degree) gap strands no inverted rest state anywhere (0 of 1,494) for 59 of the 576 placements; no two-step (30 degree) gap placement does better than 3 of 458 within 90 degrees of home and about 180 to 280 anywhere.** The width of the gap decides it, not the travel: a strut can stop 15 degrees short of a full turn and still reach every state.

**The working stances rule out most of the 59 (my reading).** The gaps must not sit in the angles the struts use for normal posing, or posing across them would need the long way round: strut A's working stances are 0 to 85 degrees below horizontal toward its side (absolute angles 275 to 360; 30 to -55 degrees from its home at 330) and strut B's are absolute 180 to 265 (-30 to +55 from home at 210; `chassis-simulation.md`). The workable pairs are: A's gap after 0 to 45 degrees from home with B's after 90 to 165; A's after 195 to 255 with B's after 315 to 15; or A's after 330 or 345 with B's after 90 to 150 (degrees from each strut's home, the gap being the step from that angle to the next 15 degrees on). The second family puts B's gap inside B's working range, and A's gaps at 0 to 30 and at 330 or 345 are inside A's; **only A's gap after 45 (the step from 45 to 60 degrees from home, absolute 15 to 30 degrees, just above horizontal toward its side) is outside A's working range, with B's gap after 105 to 165.**

**Choice (mine, 2026-11-14): a stop with travel of about 345 degrees: strut A's dead sector between absolute 15 and 30 degrees (centred on 22.5 degrees, at most about 10 degrees wide) and strut B's between absolute 345 and 360 degrees (centred on 352.5 degrees), the same 10 degree gap in each.** B's gap after 135 degrees from its home (210) is the step from absolute 345 to 360, outside B's working range of 180 to 265 and inside the table's workable set for A's gap after 45. A pin and tab can leave a dead sector as narrow as the sum of their widths: 1.5 mm for the tab and 1.0 mm for the pin on the boss rim, radius 15 mm, is 2.5 mm, 9.5 degrees (my arithmetic), which fits inside one 15 degree step with margin; the strut boss's rim or the housing flange (radius 13 mm) can carry the stop. **The earlier "firmware must return the struts to within 90 degrees of home" rule is no longer needed for righting;** a firmware soft limit 3 degrees inside each side of the gap, and the rule that a strut never commands a move through its gap, still apply. The cable (item 6) now needs the wrap for 345 degrees: 100 mm of cable, from the table there.
**Why the hard stop at all:** a servo at 0.17 N.m on a 16.5 mm boss pulls a taut wire with about 10 N, about the breaking load of a 30 AWG strand (ASM), so the servo's own torque limit is not a safe protection.
**Checked again on a 5 degree grid (`hardware/strut/stop_gap_fine.py`, 13,836 rest states, 54,064 moves; the grid angles inside a 10 degree dead sector are removed and the moves into or across them blocked):** the design above (A's sector centred on 22.5 degrees, B's on 352.5) strands **0 of 12,269 inverted rest states**, and of the 5,184 placements of the two sectors, **537 strand none**. **But A's 22.5 degrees sits at the edge of the workable range** (the workable centres for strut A, with B near 352.5, are 2.5 to 22.5 degrees; 27.5 strands some), so a few degrees of tolerance in the stop would leave it. **Revised choice: strut A's dead sector centred on 12.5 degrees (about 7.5 to 17.5 absolute) and strut B's centred on 342.5 (about 337.5 to 347.5)**, the middle of the workable ranges (A 2.5 to 22.5 for B in 302.5 to 22.5; B 302.5 to 22.5, so 80 degrees of room for B and 20 for A, centred), each 10 degrees wide at most; both are outside the working ranges (A 275 to 360 below horizontal, B 180 to 265; A's sector starts 7.5 degrees above horizontal). The pin and tab (2.5 mm at a 15 mm radius, 9.5 degrees) fit. **What is not covered:** the search moves one strut by one grid step at a time, as the simulation's righting does, and treats the body's settling as a rolling downhill in the planar model; the real stop's tolerance and the servo's stopping accuracy are not in it.

## 8. The lids and their screws: a finding, and the recommendation

**Finding:** each lid is almost all cell. The cell pocket is about 134.4 x 133.9 mm in a 140 x 140 mm face (`body-redesign.md`), leaving a **rim of only 2.8 mm (sides) and 3.05 mm (top and bottom)**. An M2 pan head is about 3.8 mm across (ASM), so screws **cannot go through the lid face**, and the rim cannot carry a gasket and a screw boss as well.
**Owner's standing decision (2026-11-12, `body-redesign.md`):** for now the lids are not separate components in the design process; the body is one block of forged carbon, and the lid joint, lid screws and board-to-board link wait until the body is more concrete.
**Recommendation (mine) when the lids come back:** retain each lid by **side-entry screws**: M2 screws going in horizontally through the body wall's outer rim into threaded inserts in the lid's edge, on the two faces that have room (the hub faces have the hub hardware in the middle but free corners; the end faces have the window and the cell pocket, so the hub faces are the better choice), eight per lid or so (ASM, to match the gasket path of about 520 mm); **seal with a flat die-cut silicone gasket, 0.5 mm (ASM), on a flange inside the rim**, dust-tight rather than watertight, to match the tabletop use case. **Alternative:** a bonded lid (not removable), which loses the cell-replacement plan the owner asked for (`body-redesign.md`, item 3 of the cell decisions). I do not recommend it.
**The owner's method for the lid's shape (2026-11-14) and a first cut of it (`hardware/body/lid_split.py`, `docs/images/lid-split.png`).** The owner's idea: once every component is placed and its cavities decided, **cut the lid out of the body along the surface equidistant from the lid components (the cells, and the board that rides on the lid) and the body components (everything else)**, a 3D Voronoi boundary that stays as far as it can from both; do it for the top and the bottom lid, overlay the two in the same orientation, and **keep only what they have in common** as the rough lid shape, before fasteners and topology optimisation. My reading: sound as a first cut, and it can be run now and rerun whenever a component moves, since the script takes about a minute (so the "everything decided first" condition makes it a check to repeat, not a step to wait for). **The prototype** works on the 1 mm voxel model of `layout.py`: lid components are the cells (a 134.4 x 133.9 mm, 1.8 mm pocket on each lid face) and the board on that lid; body components are the servos and hub with their clearance, the battery, camera, LED ring, ranging sensor, comms patch and the end-face cells; each point goes to the nearer group by a 3D Euclidean distance transform; to make a lid that lifts straight off, only the part connected to the lid face down each column is kept (a height field). **Results (the design space of each lid, not its material):** the top lid's region is **240 cm3; none of it is undercut** (0%); **the bottom lid mirrored is the same 240 cm3 and the two differ by 0 cm3**, so the identical-lids condition holds exactly for this layout and the intersection is the whole region; the lid is **4 mm deep at its shallowest, 11 mm at the median and 30 mm at its deepest** (down to the mid-plane, in empty regions far from any component); and the split surface is smooth (neighbouring columns differ by more than 3 mm in 0.2% of places). **What the method does not give (my reading):** (1) **the parting line on the outside wanders**: at the outer faces the lid's depth runs from 4 to 27 mm (median 10), so the visible seam and the gasket path would rise and fall round the body; a seal wants a clean, simple path; (2) **the surface in empty space has no structural meaning**: halfway between two components is arbitrary where nothing is there, so the deep parts (up to 30 mm) are only the method's neutral choice; (3) **the end-face cells stop it near the ends**: they span z = 7.7 to 52.3 on the faces, so at the +y and -y faces the parting line must lie between 52.3 and the cells' lid-face pocket, a lid depth under 7.7 mm there; and the camera bezel (z 12 to 48) sits below that. **Recommendation (mine): use the method for the interior, with the outer rim fixed by rule:** a parting line at a constant height round all four sides (about 6 to 7 mm below the lid face, above the end cells' top edge at 52.3, flat so that the gasket is flat), the Voronoi surface only inside that rim, and the lid keeps its board in a pocket on its underside (the board's lower face is 11.6 mm below the face, so the lid is at least that deep over the board). **Then the lid screws and the gasket:** the side-entry screws recommended above need a lid edge at least 4 mm deep at the hub faces, which the rim rule gives. **Next step if the owner agrees:** add the rim rule to `lid_split.py`, report the lid's mass at a given wall thickness, and put the parting surface into the block as a real split.
**Also needed for the removable lid:** a board-to-board connector at the joint (`body-redesign.md`, "Two lids"), and the cells' wiring through it.
