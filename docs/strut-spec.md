# Struts

Spec and design record for `hardware/strut/strut.py` (run after `hardware/body/body.py`).

Four struts, two per side on the stud's axle stub. The sources give two numbers: **70 mm from the pivot
centre to the wheel axle centre**, and **65 mm wheels**. Everything else here is a placeholder choice.
- **Layers (|x| from the body centre):** stud flange face 72.25; inboard strut A boss 73.25 to 80.25 (7 mm); outboard strut B boss 81.25 to
  88.25; the stud's axle stub ends at 92.25, leaving 4 mm for a retainer. Each boss is 30 mm across with a 12.1 mm bore on the 12 mm axle.
- **Arms:** 3 mm plate, tapering from the 30 mm boss to an 18 mm axle boss, 70.00 mm between centres. Plate A is flush with the outer face of
  boss A and plate B with the inner face of boss B, 1 mm apart. Each arm carries a 6 mm wheel-axle stub out to the wheel plane.
- **Wheel envelopes:** the script uses plain 65 mm cylinders, 44.6 mm wide (inner face to the cone apex), for its pose checks (they overlap only their own axle stubs); `hardware/wheel/wheel.py` models the real wheel.
- **Stance:** the default pose has the arms 25 degrees below horizontal, a choice and not a sourced figure. The wheel bottoms are then at
  z = -32.1 (the box floor is 32 mm above the ground), axle spacing 126.9 mm and overall length 191.9 mm. With the arms
  horizontal the overall length is 205 mm, which fits JPL's "only 20cm long" caption.
- **Passing:** the script finds interference between strut A and strut B only when they point the same way (12 of 144 sampled angle
  pairs, all at equal angles), because each strut's wheel-axle stub crosses the other's layer at the tip. So the struts cannot swap
  places, which matches Tunstel's note that same-side struts cannot pass each other. That comes out of this layout; it is not a measured
  feature of the original.
- **Clearances:** no overlap between the struts and the casting, stud or nut at the default pose. The wheel envelopes overlap only their own axle stubs.
- **Mass:** about 27 g per strut in 6061 with the 6 mm axle stub (`TIP_MODE = "axle"`), 109 g for four; **23 g each, 93 g for four in the default `TIP_MODE = "motor"`** (no stub, a bore for the spindle tube, a 24 mm tip boss).
- **Track width:** the sources give none. With the wheel stack and collar outboard of the struts the overall width is 275.7 mm; the wheel's inboard end fits the photo (see `wheel-spec.md`), but the strut layout around it is a placeholder.
- **Tip mode (layout C, 2026-11-11).** `strut.py` has `TIP_MODE`: `"motor"` (default) replaces the 6 mm axle stub with a **19.1 mm round bore** through each tip boss and plate (0.05 mm radial clearance, filled with thermal paste or adhesive so the strut is the heat sink; see `drivetrain-spec.md`) and builds the wheel support as separate parts, all coaxial with the wheel, clamped by the strut tip; the tip boss is now **24 mm** (it was 18) to leave 2.4 mm of wall. `"axle"` keeps the earlier model. Strut mass is 23 g each, 93 g for four.
- **Wheel support (designed 2026-11-11).** The Pololu #3068's shaft is only 9 mm long and Pololu gives a practical radial limit of 0.25 to 0.5 kgf, against 0.25 kgf of static wheel load, so the motor does not carry the wheel. Instead (dimensions are my choices):
  - **Spindle tube** (`out/wheel_spindles.step`, 6061, 9.7 g each): OD 19 / ID 16.2 mm (the motor is 15.6 mm across its diagonal) from the motor's tail at |x| = 77.25 (the inboard face of strut A's plate, shared by both struts) to a 1.5 mm cap at 113.5, then an 11 mm **barrel** to 124.8 with an 8 mm bearing bore. The wheel's end-plate hole is 20.5 mm around the tube.
  - **Two 693-size ball bearings** (3 x 8 x 4 mm, `out/wheel_bearings.step`) at |x| 114 to 118 and 120 to 124, mass 0.6 g each and rating "of the order of hundreds of newtons" are typical values, not looked up.
  - **Wheel drive** (`out/wheel_motors.step`): the #3068 as a 10 x 12 x 25 mm box with its tail at 77.25, a **3 mm stub shaft** from the motor's front face (102.25) through the bearings to the wheel's apex boss and collar (136.85), and an **assumed** 8 x 9 mm flexible coupling between the motor shaft and the stub shaft inside the tube (1.0 g; the real part and its length are not chosen).
  - **Loads:** the wheel's ground contact is about 11.5 mm inboard of the bearing pair's centre. At 1.0 kg (2.45 N per wheel) a bearing sees about 6 N, and about 35 N in a 6 x bump (my assumed factor); the 3 mm steel stub shaft sees about 11 MPa static and 64 MPa in a bump. The loads are small; the geometry (a 15.6 mm motor in a tube) sets the size, not the load.
  - **Checks:** no overlap between the motor drive, spindles, bearings, struts, body, studs and wheels; one strut's whole support against the other strut gives 0 of 144 angle pairs (30 degree steps); `strut.py` with both supports finds the 12 equal-angle pairs as before. The tail is 7.3 mm from the body face and 8.0 mm inboard of the wheel's inner face. Added mass per wheel: spindle 9.7 g + bearings 1.2 g + coupling 1.0 g + stub shaft 1.9 g = 13.8 g, 55 g for four.
  - **Open:** how the wheel is fixed to the stub shaft (a D-flat and set screw, say), how the spindle tube is retained in the tip bore, the coupling part, a seal against dust, a lighter tube (a 0.6 mm wall would save about 4.5 g per wheel, about 18 g for four; not modelled), and the tube's tolerance on the motor's rectangular profile.
- **Isogrid on the arm plates (experiment, off by default: `ISOGRID`, `hardware/drivetrain/isogrid_study.py`, 2026-11-11).** Triangular pockets (8 mm cells, 1.5 mm ribs, 1.8 mm deep, 2.5 mm from the edges and bosses) cut from the inboard face of each plate only, 18 per plate. Result: **2.2 g saved for the four struts (2 %)**, because the plate is only 3 mm thick and about 24 mm wide; the metal cross-section keeps 93 to 97 %; arm conduction goes from 3.6 to 3.8 K/W (neutral); but the out-of-plane bending stiffness falls to **16 %** and the stress from a 5 N sideways knock at the tip rises from about 10 to about 96 MPa (factor of safety 25 down to 3 against 6061-T6 yield, my assumed load). Not worth it at this size. Better places to take mass out: the pivot bosses (about 11 g of each strut's 23 g, 4 mm of the boss is a plain disc), and the wheels and body.
- **Not modelled:** the strut motors, the pivot bearings, the encoders, how the wheel and the spindle tube are retained, the real arm outline, strut stops.
