# Struts

**Hub (2026-11-11):** `strut.py` now defaults to `HUB_MODE = "coaxial"`: the two struts of a side turn on concentric shafts driven by servos in the body (`hardware/hub/hub.py`, `docs/drivetrain-spec.md`); the pivot bores are 15.05 mm (strut A, on a hollow shaft) and 5.05 mm (strut B, on the inner shaft), and the stationary 12 mm stud described below is the earlier arrangement (`HUB_MODE = "stud"`). Run order: body, hub, strut, wheel, assembly.

Spec and design record for `hardware/strut/strut.py` (run after `hardware/body/body.py`).

Four struts, two per side on the stud's axle stub. The sources give two numbers: **70 mm from the pivot
centre to the wheel axle centre**, and **65 mm wheels**. Everything else here is a placeholder choice.
- **Layers (|x| from the body centre):** stud flange face 72.25; inboard strut A boss 73.25 to 80.25 (7 mm); outboard strut B boss 81.25 to
  88.25; the stud's axle stub ends at 92.25, leaving 4 mm for a retainer. Each boss is 30 mm across with a 12.1 mm bore on the 12 mm axle.
- **Arms:** 3 mm plate, tapering from the 30 mm boss to an 18 mm axle boss, 70.00 mm between centres. Plate A is flush with the outer face of
  boss A and plate B with the inner face of boss B, 1 mm apart. Each arm carries a 6 mm wheel-axle stub out to the wheel plane.
- **Wheel envelopes:** the script uses plain 65 mm cylinders, 44.6 mm wide (inner face to the cone apex), for its pose checks (they overlap only their own axle stubs); `hardware/wheel/wheel.py` models the real wheel.
- **Stance:** the default pose has the arms 25 degrees below horizontal, a choice and not a sourced figure. The wheel bottoms are then at
  z = -32.1 (the box floor is 32 mm above the ground), axle spacing 126.9 mm and overall length 191.9 mm. With the arms
  horizontal the overall length is 205 mm, which fits JPL's "only 20cm long" caption.
- **Passing:** the script finds interference between strut A and strut B only when they point the same way (12 of 144 sampled angle
  pairs, all at equal angles), because each strut's wheel-axle stub crosses the other's layer at the tip. So the struts cannot swap
  places, which matches Tunstel's note that same-side struts cannot pass each other. That comes out of this layout; it is not a measured
  feature of the original.
- **Clearances:** no overlap between the struts and the casting, stud or nut at the default pose. The wheel envelopes overlap only their own axle stubs.
- **Mass:** about 27 g per strut in 6061 with the 6 mm axle stub (`TIP_MODE = "axle"`), 109 g for four; **23 g each, 93 g for four in the default `TIP_MODE = "motor"`** (no stub, a bore for the spindle tube, a 24 mm tip boss).
- **Track width:** the sources give none. With the wheel stack and collar outboard of the struts the overall width is 275.7 mm; the wheel's inboard end fits the photo (see `wheel-spec.md`), but the strut layout around it is a placeholder.
- **Tip mode (layout C, 2026-11-11).** `strut.py` has `TIP_MODE`: `"motor"` (default) replaces the 6 mm axle stub with a **19.1 mm round bore** through each tip boss and plate (0.05 mm radial clearance, filled with thermal paste or adhesive so the strut is the heat sink; see `drivetrain-spec.md`) and builds the wheel support as separate parts, all coaxial with the wheel, clamped by the strut tip and starting flush with that strut's plate; the tip boss is now **24 mm** (it was 18) to leave 2.4 mm of wall. `"axle"` keeps the earlier model. Strut mass is 23 g each, 93 g for four.
- **Wheel support (designed 2026-11-11, revised the same day).** The Pololu #3068's shaft is only 9 mm long and Pololu gives a practical radial limit of 0.25 to 0.5 kgf, against 0.25 kgf of static wheel load, so the motor does not carry the wheel. Instead (dimensions are my choices):
  - **Each strut's support starts flush with the inboard face of its own plate** (strut A at |x| = 77.25, strut B at 81.25), so nothing sticks out behind a plate toward the body. (The first version put both tails at 77.25, which left strut B's tube and motor standing 4 mm proud behind its plate; the owner pointed this out.) Strut A's tail is 7.3 mm from the body face.
  - **Spindle tube** (`out/wheel_spindles.step`, 6061, 9.9 g for A and 9.1 g for B): OD 19 / ID 16.2 mm (the motor is 15.6 mm across its diagonal) from the plate's inboard face to a 1.5 mm cap whose outer face is at |x| = 116.25 (the same for both struts), then an 11 mm **barrel** to 124.5 with an 8 mm bearing bore. The wheel's end-plate hole is 20.5 mm around the tube.
  - **Two 693-size ball bearings** (3 x 8 x 4 mm, `out/wheel_bearings.step`) side by side at |x| 116.25 to 120.25 and 120.25 to 124.25 (no spacer, so the span between their centres is 4 mm); mass 0.6 g each and a rating of "the order of hundreds of newtons" are typical values, not looked up.
  - **Wheel drive** (`out/wheel_motors.step`): the #3068 as a 10 x 12 x 25 mm box flush with the tube's tail, a **3 mm stub shaft** from the motor's front face through the bearings to the wheel's apex boss and collar (136.85), and an **assumed** 8 mm diameter, 7.5 mm long flexible coupling between the motor shaft and the stub shaft, 0.5 mm from the motor and 0.5 mm short of the cap. **That length is tight**: real 3 mm to 3 mm couplings are usually 10 mm or more, so this may need a different part or a rigid collar.
  - **Loads:** the wheel's ground contact is about 12.8 mm inboard of the bearing pair's centre. At 1.0 kg (2.45 N per wheel) a bearing sees about 9 N, and about 53 N in a 6 x bump (my assumed factor); the 3 mm steel stub shaft sees about 12 MPa static and 70 MPa in a bump. The loads are small; the geometry (a 15.6 mm motor in a tube) sets the size, not the load.
  - **Checks:** no overlap between any two of the 30 solids (struts, motors with coupler and shaft, spindles, bearings, wheels, studs and nuts) at the default pose; one strut's whole support against the other strut gives 0 of 144 angle pairs (30 degree steps); `strut.py` with both supports finds the 12 equal-angle pairs as before. Added mass per wheel: spindle 9.5 g (mean) + bearings 1.2 g + coupling 1.0 g + stub shaft 1.9 g = 13.6 g, 54 g for four.
  - **Open:** how the wheel is fixed to the stub shaft (a D-flat and set screw, say), how the spindle tube is retained in the tip bore, the real coupling part, a seal against dust, a lighter tube (a 0.6 mm wall would save about 4.5 g per wheel, about 18 g for four; not modelled), and the tube's tolerance on the motor's rectangular profile.
- **Isogrid on the arm plates (experiment, off by default: `ISOGRID`, `hardware/drivetrain/isogrid_study.py`, 2026-11-11).** Triangular pockets (8 mm cells, 1.5 mm ribs, 1.8 mm deep, 2.5 mm from the edges and bosses) cut from the inboard face of each plate only, 18 per plate. Result: **2.0 g saved for the four struts (2 %)**, because the plate is only 3 mm thick and about 24 mm wide; the metal cross-section keeps 93 to 97 %; arm conduction goes from 3.6 to 3.8 K/W (neutral); but the out-of-plane bending stiffness falls to **16 %** and the stress from a 5 N sideways knock at the tip rises from about 10 to about 96 MPa (factor of safety 25 down to 3 against 6061-T6 yield, my assumed load). Not worth it at this size. Better places to take mass out: the pivot bosses (about 11 g of each strut's 23 g, 4 mm of the boss is a plain disc), and the wheels and body.
- **Not modelled:** the strut motors, the pivot bearings, the encoders, how the wheel and the spindle tube are retained, the real arm outline, strut stops.
